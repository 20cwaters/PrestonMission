How to write him

Email — the easy way

Missionaries get one preparation day a week, and that is when they read and answer email. Write any day you like; he will see it on his next P-day.

Email him

Letters and packages

Physical mail goes to the mission office, and they pass it along at transfers or zone conferences, so give it a few weeks. Packages to the Philippines can be slow and are sometimes taxed — small, flat, and cheap travels best.

A few tips. Keep it upbeat and newsy — he only gets a few hours to read and reply. Tell him what is happening at home, ask about the people he is teaching, and send pictures. Do not be discouraged if the reply is short; some weeks the internet café closes early.

For anyone new to this

Missionary life, briefly

What is the MTC?

The Missionary Training Center. Missionaries learning a new language usually spend about six weeks at the one in Provo, Utah, then continue at a training center in their assigned country — for Preston, that is the one in Manila — before heading out to their actual mission.

What is a transfer?

Roughly every six weeks the mission president reassigns missionaries. Preston may stay put or be moved to a new town, and he may get a new companion either way. Transfer news usually shows up in the weekly email, and this site gets updated when it does.

What is a companion?

Missionaries are always paired up. Companions live, study, and teach together for the length of a transfer or longer. You will see a lot of names come and go over two years.

What is a P-day?

Preparation day — one day a week for laundry, groceries, sightseeing, and writing home. That is when the weekly email arrives, and when he is allowed to call or video chat with his family.
